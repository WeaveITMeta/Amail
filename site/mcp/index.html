The process

Four parts of a complete pipeline.

This is the general mechanism. An agent reaches real systems through the Model Context Protocol, coordinates the steps of one job, and keeps a record that the next step can verify. A person can stand at a checkpoint you configure. The rest of the pipeline can run through.

  1. 1

    Connect real tools

    An MCP server exposes tools with a name, the inputs they accept, and the result they return. You attach the servers that hold the work: mail, files, a calendar, a ledger, an issue tracker, or another system the job actually uses. The agent lists that set, then calls one tool to do one operation. The connection is the tool list you attached.

    tools/list → tools/call
  2. 2

    Coordinate the steps

    A crew shares one job. One role classifies the incoming work, another gathers what the next action needs, another prepares the outcome. Each step receives the record of the steps before it, so the handoff is a written state, and the next agent continues that same job.

    step record → next role
  3. 3

    Record and verify

    Every call writes a durable record: which tool ran, what it was given, and what came back. That record is the state of the run. A later step checks its inputs against the record before it continues, and a run that stops can resume from the last completed record.

    tool, input, result
  4. 4

    A person at the checkpoints you choose

    Human review is a quality gate you attach to a checkpoint. The checkpoint has a concrete artifact: a reply ready to send, a ledger entry, a filing, or another action you want a person to see. With the gate set, the pipeline pauses, the person checks that artifact, and the run continues or returns to the previous step. Leave the gate unset and that step completes with the rest of the pipeline.

    checkpoint: review

Quality gates

Review is available where the outcome deserves it.

End-to-end automation stays available. You station a person on the checkpoints that matter for that pipeline, and the other steps finish on their own. On the mail path, approval before a send is one gate of this kind: it checks the exact draft, and the steps around it still do their work.

  • Tools you attach. The agent calls the MCP tools on the servers you connect for that job.
  • A crew, one job. Each role reads the prior record and adds the next step.
  • A record per call. Tool name, inputs, and result stay with the run so a later step can verify them.
  • A gate you can set. Turn review on for a checkpoint, or leave that step in the automated path.
  • A finished outcome. The pipeline ends with the record of what ran and, where a gate was set, who released it.
